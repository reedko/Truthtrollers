# CF2 V6-C30 candidate-cap findings

## Controlled change

V6-C30 retains the V6 architecture and models. It changes:

- Call A candidate prose ceiling: 18 to 30;
- Call A schema `maxItems`: 18 to 30;
- Call B schema/validator capacity: 18 to 30.

The host portfolio maximum remains 12. Candidate capacity is not yet
article-length-dependent.

Default V6 remains C18/P12 and byte-identical to the protected V5 Call A.

## Initial strict-validation run

Call A succeeded on all fixtures:

| Fixture | Call A candidates |
|---|---:|
| CF1-F01 | 30 |
| CF1-F02 | 29 |
| CF1-F03 | 30 |

Each complete run then failed because one invalid Call B candidate invalidated
the entire batch:

- F01: omitted attribution layer;
- F02: ungrounded supplier;
- F03: omitted attribution layer.

This showed that all-or-nothing semantic validation becomes increasingly brittle
as the number of independently judged candidates grows.

## Candidate-quarantine run

The C30 arm was rerun with candidate-level quarantine. Default V6 remains strict.

| Fixture | Candidates | Valid B | Quarantined | Final | Time | Tokens |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CF1-F01 | 30 | 24 | 6 | 4 | 41.5 s | 30,488 |
| CF1-F02 | 26 | 18 | 8 | 12 | 41.4 s | 18,744 |
| CF1-F03 | 30 | 30 | 0 | 12 | 45.7 s | 35,397 |

### F03

C30 recovered in one final portfolio:

- all three Jefferson County Public Health opponent assertions;
- William Thompson as supplier of the CDC data-manipulation assertion;
- later aluminum and thimerosal assertions.

The three JCPH assertions remained `weakens -> invert`. This is evidence that the
old 18-candidate ceiling caused real zero-sum competition among argument clusters.

The unresolved V6 failure remains: every F03 `articleTreatment` was `reported`.

### F01

Discovery reached the substantive results and discussion, but Call B labeled 20
of 24 valid assertions `no_effect`. Only four assertions survived the existing
portfolio relevance filter. Candidate capacity did not cause this under-selection;
thesis-effect judgment did.

### F02

Eight candidates were quarantined, mostly because explicit Antoniou reporting
frames were returned without the required attribution layer. The remaining
portfolio reached 12, but lost useful named-source assertions. Candidate capacity
exposed incomplete attribution decomposition.

## Conclusion

C30 improves discovery headroom and produced the strongest F03 combination so
far. It does not by itself solve downstream thesis-effect, article-treatment, or
attribution-decomposition errors.

The experiment supports keeping separate budgets:

- a larger discovery-candidate ceiling;
- a smaller final portfolio ceiling.

It does not yet establish an article-length formula. More fixtures are needed
before replacing the fixed C30 ceiling with a dynamic budget.

## Treatment-fallback replay

The exact saved C30 Call A and Call B outputs were replayed with this host-only
selection policy:

1. retain non-`no_effect` judgments;
2. retain `challenged + no_effect`;
3. when the portfolio is still under 12, fill only from `adopted + no_effect`;
4. never rescue `reported + no_effect`;
5. preserve the original labels and record `selectionBasis`.

F01 expanded from 4 to 12 without rerunning Calls A/B. The fallback recovered
the five central judgments that the narrow thesis had incorrectly excluded:

- the 93.4% versus 90.6% 36-month comparison;
- its 1.49 odds ratio and confidence interval;
- the stronger association among children aged 3–5;
- preexisting developmental delay among late-vaccinated cases;
- the early-intervention enrollment explanation.

It also selected three less useful adopted facts about MADDSP identification,
prevalence, and sample size. The policy therefore improves recall but does not
repair the erroneous thesis-effect labels or fully solve portfolio precision.

F02 and F03 were already full; their selected candidate IDs were unchanged.
